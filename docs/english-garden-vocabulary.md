# English garden vocabulary

## How each game uses the data

| Game | Data per session | Learning support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nối hình với từ | 3 pairs at 3–4, 4 pairs at older levels; picture and word columns shuffled separately | Explicit listen buttons on both sides; family pictures include Vietnamese relationship labels |
| Bức tranh bí mật | Up to 6 unique targets, 9 reveal tiles, 3 unique word options per target | Vietnamese clue, optional word audio, picture reveal at the child's pace, example after success |
| Ong chăm chỉ đánh vần | Up to 6 targets, 1/2/3 missing letters by level, indexed correct letters and 2 decoys | Visible letters, pictogram, Vietnamese meaning/clue, listen, reset and retry; repeated letters remain independent |

The shared vocabulary is the source of truth; game-specific questions are generated by pure helpers rather than copied into three divergent lists. Topic/age pools cap session length when fewer than six words are available. Level labels describe scaffolding, not a developmental assessment. No game requires an external service, microphone or keyboard typing.

The three garden games share 64 curated words in `src/lib/englishGardenGames.ts`. Each record contains a stable topic-prefixed ID, lowercase English word, Vietnamese meaning, local Unicode emoji, a short clue in each language, an English example and minimum age band. No remote image, network request or generated asset is required.

Age bands are cumulative scaffolding levels, not developmental assessments. Every topic has four words available at 3–4, six at 4–5 and eight at 5–6. Older children can revisit all younger vocabulary. The `all` topic therefore offers 32, 48 and 64 words respectively.

Emoji appearance varies between devices. A pictogram represents the named object without implying that a particular clothing style or activity belongs to a particular gender. Family examples describe relationships without assuming every child lives with the same relatives. `Doll` uses a nesting-doll pictogram; `cousin` uses a child pictogram and relies on its relationship clue. Listen and written words remain available when a picture alone cannot communicate a relationship. The scissors example explicitly includes adult assistance.

`getGardenWords(topic, age)` filters by topic and cumulative age. Unknown topics return an empty list. `makeGardenRound` samples unique English answers without replacement, caps the requested count to available words and leaves its input unchanged. `makeGardenOptions` always includes the target exactly once, excludes duplicate spellings and returns fewer choices when the pool is small. Its default is three options. These helpers do not award rewards or persist progress.

`makeBeePuzzle` accepts alphabetic English words of at least three letters and returns uppercase answer, slots and letter choices. It hides one letter at 3–4, two at 4–5 and three at 5–6, leaving at least one visible letter. Choices contain exactly enough correct letters for the missing slots plus two distinct decoy letters. Neither decoy occurs among the missing letters, so even a one-blank puzzle asks the child to choose. Each correct choice ID includes its original letter position, so repeated letters such as the three A's in BANANA are independent buttons; decoy IDs use `decoy-X` with their letter. IDs are scoped to a puzzle. All randomness is injectable for deterministic tests; `shuffle` uses Fisher–Yates and returns a fresh array.

Regression coverage in `tests/englishGardenGames.test.ts` checks corpus completeness, unique IDs and words, topic/age coverage, immutable and deterministic sampling, exactly-once answers, small pools, missing-letter counts, visible scaffolding, repeated-letter choice IDs and rejection of nonalphabetic Bee input.
